Toll Family Playground: Green Gazebos and Steel Springs

Central Park near the W. 85th St. entrance (Mariners' Gate) off Central Park West, Manhattan
Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, November 8, 2026 | Filed under: Best for Tots
Steel springy things and the green gazebos.

Yet another playground along Central Park West, funded by generous donors. In this case, the Toll Family. Who are the Tolls? Generous donors, identity unknown.

It has good bones. It's secluded, it has only one entry point, and it's surrounded by a nice fence. Kids can climb over a mini fence to play in some woods, which is fun because they get to feel like wild little rebels. But fear not: they're still trapped in the ring.

It was very busy on an autumn Sunday afternoon, with one private birthday party going on. The crowd is Upper West Side locals. Tourists don't usually wander in, because it's a bit off the main loop.

Baby Swing City. Only baby swings, and a ton of them.

My daughter is becoming a playground reviewer herself. She walked in, looked around, and said: “I've never seen a playground before that has only baby swings… what the heck?” It's true. For some strange reason, this playground only has baby swings, the little round black basket kind. And a ton of them. Why not mix it up a bit, Tolls?

There are some unique springy and spinny stainless steel things here, and that's what brought us in. I'd take a classic seesaw over these modern spring ones, but kids still seem to like them. Quite a few of the spring toys only fit one kid, which leads to some fights. The four-person mini spring seesaw lets instant friendships form. Unless some quadruplets are hogging it. My daughter loved the stainless steel spinners. She would spin herself until she got so dizzy she fell off, then repeat.

The modern spring seesaw.
The stainless steel spinner, a dizzy favorite.

There is exactly one thing here for grown-ups: a steel disc you stand on and wiggle to send the balls rolling around the rings. Nothing like wiggling your balls in front of a crowded playground.

The one item for adults. Stand on it and wiggle.

It's a very good playground for parking your butt on a bench and keeping your kid in sight. You can really see them everywhere (OK, maybe not inside the tube slide). To me, that's also a downside, because it means there aren't any large or fun obstacles. It's not really a fun playground for adults, not that any are designed to be, but as you may have picked up on, I like to play a bit. There's a lot of open space for kids to run around. The splash pad is pretty basic, but hey, it has one.

Not a bad playground, just small in scale. I think 2 to 3 is the prime age for this one. I didn't love it, but my daughter really did: it was her age group, with age-appropriate equipment.

No bathrooms nearby. Like, none within walking distance that I can think of.

Verdict: a clean, secluded, fenced-in playground for the little ones, with fun steel springs and spinners. Great for toddlers and bench-sitting parents. Bigger kids (and dads) may get bored.
