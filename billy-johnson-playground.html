The Secret Garden of Slides: Billy Johnson Playground

Central Park, near the E. 67th St. entrance off Fifth Ave., Manhattan
Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, September 13, 2026 | Filed under: Hidden Gems
The little stone bridge, with the sneaky water jets underneath.

Here's one many Manhattan parents have walked right past. Billy Johnson Playground is tucked into Central Park near East 67th Street, and you can't see it from Fifth Avenue or from the jogging loop. I've mentioned it to plenty of fellow parents who had no idea it existed. If Heckscher is too busy or just not your style, this is the place to go — it's sort of the East Side's answer to the Adventure Playground on the West Side.

The granite slide, set into the rocks.

It was designed back in the 1980s to feel like a miniature version of the park around it, and it shows. There's no truly original equipment here, but there's a picturesque little stone bridge (a kid-sized cousin of Gapstow Bridge down at the Pond), a long granite slide set right into a rocky hill, and a nicer-than-average splash pad. Regulars bring a piece of cardboard to sit on for extra speed on the slide. Take the tip.

Making new friends on the tire swing.

The best trick is under the bridge. Water jets are built into the pathway there, which makes a fun little stretch to run through over and over again. And there's always a certain kind of kid — I was one of them — who waits for some unsuspecting soul to stroll under the bridge, then hits the button and cackles as the jets go off. It never stops being funny.

The splash pad, flagstones and all.

The tire swing is where kids learn to share and make friends. There's also a nice flat, open sandy area that's perfect for a game of kickball. Line of sight isn't great with all the rocks and plantings, but there's only one entrance and exit, so there's nothing to worry about.

Verdict: a genuine hidden gem. I'm not sure I'd cross the city just for it, but if you're anywhere on the East Side, it's a lovely way to spend an afternoon.

The Stump Report

Overall Fun:Granite slide, tire swing, and those sneaky bridge jets.
Originality:Nothing truly original, but the rustic stone-bridge setting is a charmer.
Cleanliness:Tidy and well tended.
Size:Approx. 20,000 sq ft, our estimate. Enough flat sandy space for a game of kickball.
Dad Test:Grown-ups fit the granite slide, and the bridge-jet prank never gets old.
Bathrooms:None nearby. Plan ahead.

The playground

On the run: it's tucked just off the Central Park loop near 67th Street, so it's easy to miss even if you run past it every week. By bike: the loop drive passes just to the west; walk your bike in, since the playground is hidden from the road. By train: N, R, or W to 5 Av/59 St, or the 6 to 68 St–Hunter College, then walk to the East 67th Street park entrance.
