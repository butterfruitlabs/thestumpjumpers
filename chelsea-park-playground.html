Chelsea Park Playground: Crazy Ladders and Too Much Plastic

Chelsea Park, W. 27th to 28th St. between Ninth and Tenth Avenues, Manhattan
Most Recently Reviewed On Saturday, November 7, 2026 | Filed under: Most Fun for Parents
Blue rails, red hoops, and a whole lot of plastic.

Chelsea Park Playground sits between the projects and a giant warehouse. It's not a playground we'd typically stumble upon, since it's not off a bike or running path.

This is a decent playground, but it has lots of plastic equipment, and if you've spent at least 30 seconds reading this blog, you know that I hate plastic equipment. It goes beyond the static shocks. One day I'll pontificate on that. For the 95% of parents who don't care whether a playground is plastic or not, there's some fun to be had here.

There are lots of interesting ways to climb up onto the equipment. My daughter remarked, “These are some crazy ladders!” There are also some interesting red hoop things to climb through, which reveal that this stuff was made by Landscape Structures, out of Minnesota.

A crazy ladder.
More crazy ladders.
The red hoops give it away: Landscape Structures, Delano, Minnesota.

You can get a pretty good game of the floor is lava going here, but WHY can't designers make a complete loop of equipment, so you can run a full circuit without melting? You can only get about halfway around this playground.

The periscopes are pretty cool, and we were excited to try them, but they're all scratched up to the point where you can barely see through them.

And the little cones you talk into: these have been in every single playground since I was a kid, yet they have never once worked. Maybe on the day they were installed. I think kids stuff things into them, they clog, and that's the end of it. How many kids have tried talking to each other across the playground, only to be disappointed?

Trying so hard to see through the scratched periscope.
Hello? Anybody? The talk tube strikes again.
From the Top of the Plastic Jungle

As we moved around, I noticed a lot of the equipment is repetitive, like it was cheaper to buy in multiples. Two of the exact same slides. Two of the exact same spinners. Two of the exact same swings… OK, I guess that one's normal, and nobody ever complains about it. But why not use the precious square footage for a variety of equipment?

Seal statues make for a cool, but not too exciting, splash pad. It's all fenced in with one gate, and visibility is good throughout, since it's flat and the equipment only blocks your view at certain angles.

The spitting seals, with the bathroom in the background.

The park bathrooms are very close to the playground (just walk past the seals), but they're just plain nasty.

The dads' playground.

There's an adult workout area just outside the playground. That's great. A dads' playground! We need more of these in the city.

The playground is part of a much larger park. Thankfully, the names aren't confusing like Clinton Park/Erie Canal Playground. This one goes with the nice, simple naming convention: Chelsea Park/Chelsea Park Playground. Also in the park are a big soccer field, the aforementioned adult workout area, basketball courts, the bathrooms, and some picnic areas.

Verdict: a decent, fenced-in, mostly plastic playground with crazy ladders, half a lava loop, and a bonus workout area for the grown-ups. Fine if you're nearby, but not worth crossing town for.

The Stump Report

Overall Fun:Decent. Crazy ladders, but only half a lava loop.
Originality:Mostly catalog plastic, with quite a few duplicates.
Cleanliness:A fair amount of trash, plus scratched-up periscopes and clogged talk tubes.
Size:About 9,000 sq ft. On the small side, and the duplicate equipment eats up space.
Bathrooms:Very close, just past the seals. Plain nasty, though.

Stump average: 2.4 out of 5. Ranked #24 of 27 NYC playgrounds we've reviewed.
