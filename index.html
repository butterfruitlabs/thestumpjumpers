No Plastic Allowed: Adventure Playground NEW!

Most Recently Reviewed On Tuesday, September 29, 2026 | Filed under: Hidden Gems
A secluded, sixty-year-old classic on a Central Park hill, built from stainless steel, concrete, and sand — without the modern plastic equipment. A long fire pole, hidey castles, a steep little slide, and an epic ash tree. A smaller, quieter Heckscher that locals love. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:A good all-around playground: fire pole, hidey castles, and a little river.
Originality:A classic 1966 design with zero plastic, but basically a smaller Heckscher.
Cleanliness:Held up remarkably well for a sixty-year-old playground. Respectful parents.
Size:Approx. 20,000 sq ft, our estimate. Mid-size: a smaller-scale Heckscher.
Dad Test:The long fire pole is fun at any age. Dads fit.
Bathrooms:A bit of a hike, at Tavern on the Green.

The OG, but Not Necessarily the GOAT: Heckscher Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Saturday, September 26, 2026 | Filed under: Worth the Trip
Known to regulars simply as “The Heck,” this is Central Park's oldest and largest playground, and the default meetup spot for half the families we know. Castle-like stone structures, a stream you can follow up to Umpire Rock, and skyscraper views. Usually packed with tourists and litter, but still very much worth a visit. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Castles, a stream to follow, and room to run.
Originality:Stone castles with a stream running through them.
Cleanliness:Dorito bags on the ground and broken glass on Umpire Rock.
Size:About 78,000 sq ft (1.8 acres). Central Park's largest playground.
Dad Test:Following the stream up Umpire Rock is fun for grown-ups too.
Bathrooms:Right in the playground, shared with the whole park.

The Secret Garden of Slides: Billy Johnson Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, September 13, 2026 | Filed under: Hidden Gems
Tucked out of sight from Fifth Avenue and the jogging loop, this rustic little playground is mostly locals, and plenty of parents don't know it exists. A granite slide, a storybook stone bridge with water jets underneath, and a tire swing that turns strangers into friends. A true hidden gem. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Granite slide, tire swing, and those sneaky bridge jets.
Originality:Nothing truly original, but the rustic stone-bridge setting is a charmer.
Cleanliness:Tidy and well tended.
Size:Approx. 20,000 sq ft, our estimate. Enough flat sandy space for a game of kickball.
Dad Test:Grown-ups fit the granite slide, and the bridge-jet prank never gets old.
Bathrooms:None nearby. Plan ahead.

Four Playgrounds, One Pier: Pier 6, Brooklyn Bridge Park

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, August 16, 2026 | Filed under: Worth the Trip & Most Fun for Parents
One of NYC's latest and greatest: a group of medium-sized playgrounds side by side that add up to an amusement park. A Water Lab built into a rocky hill (with an Archimedes screw!), steel slides, secret bamboo trails, and a serene swing valley. Very busy, but definitely worth a day trip. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Four playgrounds in one, like a little amusement park.
Originality:An Archimedes screw, bamboo trails, and a rocky-hill Water Lab.
Cleanliness:Well kept for how busy it gets.
Size:About 70,000 sq ft (1.6 acres). Four separate play zones to explore.
Dad Test:The Water Lab, steel slides, and plenty for grown-ups on the pier.
Bathrooms:On the pier by the Water Lab; a hike from the slides.

The New Pier 97: Needs a Better Maintenance Team

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, August 9, 2026
A brand-new playground right on the Hudson with a big slide you can only reach by conquering a tall ropes course. Fun, and nicely scary. Sadly, a lot of the new equipment is already broken, the bathrooms are always closed, and the staff seem to be on a permanent break. Go for the slide, then move on. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:The ropes-course slide is a thrill. The rest is hit or miss.
Originality:A slide you have to earn is a nice change of pace.
Cleanliness:Garbage in the sand and broken equipment everywhere.
Size:About 6,875 sq ft. Compact, but the pier adds a ball field and a lawn.
Dad Test:The all-ages granite slide and ropes course work for grown-ups too.
Bathrooms:Very close by, but always closed.

Imagination Required: Imagination Playground at Burling Slip

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, August 2, 2026 | Filed under: Best Avoided
Deserted on a beautiful Sunday, and the famous big foam building blocks were nowhere to be found. The interesting-looking pipes don't do anything. Maybe that's the joke: the only thing left to play with is your imagination. The boat theme and Seaport setting are nice, but that's about it. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:No blocks, and the pipes don't do anything.
Originality:The ship theme suits the Seaport. That's the highlight.
Cleanliness:Not filthy, just forgotten.
Size:Approx. 18,000 sq ft, our estimate. A decent footprint with very little to fill it.
Dad Test:Nothing to do for kids, let alone grown-ups.
Bathrooms:Right inside the playground; only moderately scuzzy.

A Waiver, a Fence, and a Pile of Glorious Junk: The Yard

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, August 2, 2026 | Filed under: Worth the Trip
Picture a vacant lot where somebody dumped every old toy, plank, and tire from trash night, then handed the whole thing over to kids. It's muddy, weedy, and gloriously scuzzy — exactly the kind of play most kids never get anymore. Sign the waiver, wave goodbye at the gate, and let them at it. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:A literal junkyard where kids get to play without their parents. Hard to top that.
Originality:Nothing else like it in the five boroughs.
Cleanliness:Mud, weeds, and junk are the whole point. Pack wipes.
Size:About 50,000 sq ft. Huge, with separate little-kid and big-kid zones.
Dad Test:Parents wait outside the fence. No dads allowed.
Bathrooms:Not nearby. Plan a stop before you get in line.

Four Slides and a Hill: Slide Hill, Governors Island

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, August 2, 2026 | Filed under: Worth the Trip & Most Fun for Parents
Just a bunch of metal slides built into the side of a hill on Governors Island, and it's great because it does that one thing very well. Short fat slides, long thin slides, and the longest slide in all of NYC. Pair it with The Yard for a perfect island day. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Four slides, three ways up, zero boredom.
Originality:Slides built right into a 40-foot hill, including the city's longest.
Cleanliness:Well kept, like the rest of the island.
Size:Approx. 15,000 sq ft of hillside, our estimate. One big hill, four slides.
Dad Test:Four slides for all sizes, including one built for two.
Bathrooms:Not nearby. Plan ahead.

A River Runs Through It: River Run Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, July 19, 2026 | Filed under: Hidden Gems & Worth the Trip & Best Hudson River Greenway Stops
An aging classic built around a squiggly concrete “river” that flows from a splash fountain down to the legendary sandbox, whose so-bad-they're-great sculptures creep some parents out. Hidden in Riverside Park, often overlooked, and completely wonderful. Both a hidden gem and worth the trip. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Floating boats down a river never gets old.
Originality:A concrete Hudson River and a sandbox full of faces.
Cleanliness:Showing its age, but well loved.
Size:Approx. 25,000 sq ft, our estimate. Plenty of room between the river and the sandbox.
Dad Test:Who doesn't want to float a boat down a river?
Bathrooms:A large public restroom right in the park.

Seesaws! (Some Assembly Required): Classic Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, July 19, 2026 | Filed under: Best Avoided
A playground full of seesaws and a real metal jungle gym — the stuff I grew up on — left to fend for itself. Flaking paint, weeds, missing seesaws, and a dead splash pad. Great concept, sad playground. The city should either give it some love or move the classics somewhere they'll be appreciated. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:The seesaws that remain still work. Bark-stomping is a bonus.
Originality:Real seesaws and a metal jungle gym are rare finds these days.
Cleanliness:Weeds, flaking paint, and a trash can blocking the splash pad.
Size:Approx. 25,000 sq ft, our estimate. Plenty of room, too bad so much of it is out of order.
Dad Test:Seesaws need a partner, and grown-ups make great counterweights.
Bathrooms:There's a restroom, but it seems to be permanently closed.

Hippos on the Jogging Loop: Safari Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Saturday, July 18, 2026
Our standard pit stop on a jogging-stroller lap around Central Park. The hippos and play canoes are genuine crowd-pleasers, though a few hippos are held together with duct tape these days. The real star is unofficial: a steep lump at the north end that beats the actual slide. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:The hippos, the canoes, and that steep mystery mound carry the day.
Originality:A hippo river is not something you'll find at the corner playground.
Cleanliness:Tidy enough, but the duct-taped hippos are due for a spa day.
Size:Approx. 20,000 sq ft, our estimate. Room to roam without losing sight of anyone.
Dad Test:The steep mound is a fun climb at any age.
Bathrooms:None on site. Plan accordingly.

Sweet Stuff on the Waterfront: Domino Park

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, June 28, 2026 | Filed under: Worth the Trip & Most Fun for Parents
Playgrounds right on the water with skyline views, clubhouse structures styled after the old sugar refinery, a mist bath that seems to rise from the rocks, and a splash pad with jets that fire at random. Spotless bathrooms, a farmers market some days, and lots for parents to do. Very high marks. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Clubhouses, slides, mist, and splash jets. Nobody wanted to leave.
Originality:A playground designed around sugar refining. Brilliant.
Cleanliness:Immaculate grounds, and bathrooms you'd actually use.
Size:About 6,500 sq ft at ground level. Compact footprint, but it climbs several stories, with more play along the water.
Dad Test:Mist bath, skyline views, a big lawn, and a farmers market.
Bathrooms:Across the street, but big private stalls and spotless.

River Run's Little Cousin: Pier 51 Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Saturday, June 6, 2026 | Filed under: Best Hudson River Greenway Stops
Like a smaller, newer, more colorful version of River Run, built on a pier. The stream through the middle is perfect for damming with sand and floating things down. Worth a stop if you're near Little Island, but it's small, and I wouldn't go out of my way. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Damming and boat-floating in the stream is a winner.
Originality:A mini River Run, on a pier.
Cleanliness:Newer, cleaner, and more colorful than most.
Size:Approx. 10,000 sq ft, our estimate. Kind of small.
Dad Test:Damming the stream is fun for a minute, but it's small.
Bathrooms:We don't know of one on the pier itself.

Granite Slides at the Tip of the Island: Battery Playscape

Most Recently Reviewed On Monday, May 25, 2026 | Filed under: Worth the Trip & Most Fun for Parents & Best Hudson River Greenway Stops
A great playground with play pieces you won't find anywhere else, and perhaps the best slides in the city — carved from granite. The little stage was a big hit, and the SeaGlass Carousel is right nearby. Highly recommended; just note that the bathrooms are closed for renovations. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Granite slides, treehouses, and a stage. Nobody was bored.
Originality:Five ecology zones and a theater built into the playground.
Cleanliness:Well tended, with some wear in the busiest spots.
Size:About 65,000 sq ft (1.5 acres). One of the biggest playgrounds in Manhattan.
Dad Test:Granite slides no parent can resist, plus a carousel nearby.
Bathrooms:Closed for renovations; a long walk to the next ones.

The Fastest Slide in Town: Chelsea Waterside Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, May 24, 2026 | Filed under: Hidden Gems & Worth the Trip & Best Hudson River Greenway Stops
One of my favorite playgrounds of all time. A giant wooden pipefish, one of the tallest (and definitely fastest) slides in the city, and a splash pad with salvaged stone bull heads and snail jets. Its only flaw is its name — it isn't actually on the water, which may be why it stays a local secret. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:That slide alone is worth the trip.
Originality:A giant wooden pipefish and stone bull fountains.
Cleanliness:Well kept, with a little wear on the fish.
Size:About 17,000 sq ft. Packs a lot into three levels of play.
Dad Test:One of the city's tallest, fastest slides. Grown-ups welcome.
Bathrooms:Just outside the playground gate.

Logs, Ropes, and a Hidden Door: Margaret L. Kempner Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, May 17, 2026
Just okay. I'd passed it a hundred times on the Central Park loop and finally went in. The log obstacles and a strange rope contraption make it worth a stop for kids who love to climb, but the entrance is hard to find and there are no bathrooms nearby. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Worth a stop for kids who love to climb.
Originality:Log obstacles and a genuinely strange rope contraption.
Cleanliness:The picnic grove doubles as a bathroom. Enough said.
Size:Approx. 30,000 sq ft, our estimate. One of Central Park's bigger playgrounds.
Dad Test:The log obstacles are a fun challenge at any age.
Bathrooms:None nearby, as the picnic grove can attest.

Extinction Event: Dinosaur Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Friday, May 8, 2026 | Filed under: Best Avoided
About half the playground is fenced off because of a sinkhole, and the half that's open is in rough shape. The fiberglass dinosaurs it's named for have holes in them and look like a high school art project. Like Classic Playground, it feels as if the city gave up. The promised renovation can't come soon enough. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Half the playground is behind a fence.
Originality:Two dinosaurs. Neither very convincing.
Cleanliness:Worn, patched, and a little sad.
Size:About 48,000 sq ft (1.1 acres). Big on paper, but roughly half is closed off by the sinkhole.
Dad Test:Half of it is closed. Nothing here for anyone.
Bathrooms:A 1930s stone restroom stands at the south end.

Two Orange Lumps and a Gate: Balsley Park

Most Recently Reviewed On Saturday, May 2, 2026 | Filed under: Best Avoided
The worst playground we've ever reviewed. The fenced-in kids' area holds two lumpy orange things, and it's usually occupied by grown-ups who are not there for the lumpy orange things. I've never seen another kid in there. Lowest ratings across the board. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Two orange lumps. We counted twice.
Originality:The lumps are orange. That's the whole theme.
Cleanliness:Not a place you want little hands touching.
Size:The whole plaza is only about 10,900 sq ft (a quarter acre). A small corner of a small plaza.
Dad Test:Two orange lumps. Enough said.
Bathrooms:None to speak of.

The Big Guy and the Little Guys: Tom Otterness Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Sunday, April 26, 2026 | Filed under: Hidden Gems & Best Hudson River Greenway Stops
A giant bronze fellow lounging in a courtyard off 42nd Street, with slides for legs — a supersized cousin of the little bronze guys in the 14th Street subway station. Clean, quiet, and one of a kind. My daughter calls it “The Big Guy and Little Guys Playground,” and I can't improve on that. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:The leg-slides are built for racing.
Originality:A playable sculpture by a famous artist. Enough said.
Cleanliness:Spotless every time we've visited.
Size:Approx. 4,000 sq ft, our estimate. Basically one big bronze guy, plus a tot lot next door.
Dad Test:Racing down the leg-slides quickly becomes a best-of-seven.
Bathrooms:None. The nearest are across 12th Ave in Hudson River Park.

Swallowed by a Sturgeon: Pier 26 Science Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Saturday, September 14, 2024 | Filed under: Worth the Trip & Best Hudson River Greenway Stops
Two giant climbable sturgeon that feel less like playground equipment and more like a sculpture you've been given permission to crawl all over. Add a fun playground next door, mini golf, an oyster bar on a boat, and serviceberries to snack on. Worth the trip — just not on a crowded weekend if you can help it. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Climbing inside a giant fish never gets old.
Originality:A climbable sculpture of an endangered Hudson River fish.
Cleanliness:Well kept by the Hudson River Park crew.
Size:About 4,000 sq ft for the sturgeon zone alone. Plus the playground, mini golf, and courts next door.
Dad Test:Parents fit inside the sturgeon, plus mini golf and oysters nearby.
Bathrooms:A public restroom right in the middle, between the fish and the playground.

Where First Steps Happen: Tarr-Coyne Tots Playground

Most Recently Reviewed On Saturday, April 20, 2024
A little toddler playground next to the Adventure Playground, with soft rubber lumps that are practically designed for first steps. We came here a lot when my daughter was learning to walk. No rowdy big kids, just calm Upper West Side parents and lots of pacifiers. Read the full review »
The Stump Report
Overall Fun:Perfect for brand-new walkers, not much beyond that.
Originality:Rubber lumps and a rubber mountain.
Cleanliness:Tidy, give or take a few dropped pacifiers.
Size:Approx. 8,000 sq ft, our estimate. A small tot lot.
Dad Test:Built for brand-new walkers. Grown-ups just watch.
Bathrooms:In Tavern on the Green, a few hundred feet away.

~*~ About The Stumpjumpers ~*~

I'm a father of two who likes to run or bike to playgrounds on the weekends, and I usually end up playing on them as much as my kids do, if not more. We ride around on a ’93 Specialized Stumpjumper that I pulled out of a dumpster and fixed up with my daughter.

Every review on this site is my own honest take, written as we visit, usually after we've been a few times so we can get a real feel for a place first. No press releases, no copy-pasta, just what it's actually like to show up with a kid (and a bike) on a weekend morning.
