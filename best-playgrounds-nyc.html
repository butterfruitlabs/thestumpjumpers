The Best Playgrounds in NYC, Ranked

Updated November 2026

Every list of the best playgrounds in NYC says the same thing: they're all great. They're not. We run or bike to every playground we review, the kid plays until she's done, and then each one gets a Stump Report score for fun, originality, cleanliness, size, and bathrooms.

This is every playground we've reviewed so far, ranked best to worst. The scores are a big part of it, but when it's close, the final order is our call. The top of the list is worth crossing a borough for. The bottom of the list is there so you know where not to bother. For more specific picks, see our guides to the best playgrounds for toddlers, Central Park playgrounds, and Hudson River Greenway stops.

Work in progress: this ranks only the playgrounds we've reviewed so far, and NYC has hundreds more. The list will grow, and the order will change, as we visit more.

  1. 1 Pier 26 Science Playground

    Hudson River Park at N. Moore St., Tribeca, Manhattan

    Two giant climbable sturgeon that feel less like playground equipment and more like a sculpture you've been given permission to crawl all over. Add a fun playground next door, mini golf, an oyster bar on a boat, and serviceberries to snack on. Worth the trip — just not on a crowded weekend if you can help it.

    Stump average: 4.6
    Overall Fun: Climbing inside a giant fish never gets old.
    Read the full review »

  2. 2 Battery Playscape

    The Battery, at the southern tip of Manhattan

    A great playground with play pieces you won't find anywhere else, and perhaps the best slides in the city — carved from granite. The little stage was a big hit, and the SeaGlass Carousel is right nearby. Highly recommended; just note that the bathrooms are closed for renovations.

    Stump average: 4.2
    Overall Fun: Granite slides, treehouses, and a stage. Nobody was bored.
    Read the full review »

  3. 3 Pier 6, Brooklyn Bridge Park

    Brooklyn Bridge Park at Atlantic Ave. & Furman St., Brooklyn Heights, Brooklyn

    One of NYC's latest and greatest: a group of medium-sized playgrounds side by side that add up to an amusement park. A Water Lab built into a rocky hill (with an Archimedes screw!), steel slides, secret bamboo trails, and a serene swing valley. Very busy, but definitely worth a day trip.

    Stump average: 4.4
    Overall Fun: Four playgrounds in one, like a little amusement park.
    Read the full review »

  4. 4 Domino Park

    River St. between Grand & S. 5th Sts., Williamsburg, Brooklyn

    Playgrounds right on the water with skyline views, clubhouse structures styled after the old sugar refinery, a mist bath that seems to rise from the rocks, and a splash pad with jets that fire at random. Spotless bathrooms, a farmers market some days, and lots for parents to do. Very high marks.

    Stump average: 4.4
    Overall Fun: Clubhouses, slides, mist, and splash jets. Nobody wanted to leave.
    Read the full review »

  5. 5 Chelsea Waterside Playground

    11th Ave. & W. 23rd St., Chelsea, Manhattan

    One of my favorite playgrounds of all time. A giant wooden pipefish, one of the tallest (and definitely fastest) slides in the city, and a splash pad with salvaged stone bull heads and snail jets. Its only flaw is its name — it isn't actually on the water, which may be why it stays a local secret.

    Stump average: 4.2
    Overall Fun: That slide alone is worth the trip.
    Read the full review »

  6. 6 River Run Playground

    Riverside Park between W. 81st & 83rd Sts., Manhattan

    An aging classic built around a squiggly concrete “river” that flows from a splash fountain down to the legendary sandbox, whose so-bad-they're-great sculptures creep some parents out. Hidden in Riverside Park, often overlooked, and completely wonderful. Both a hidden gem and worth the trip.

    Stump average: 4.4
    Overall Fun: Floating boats down a river never gets old.
    Read the full review »

  7. 7 Heckscher Playground

    Central Park, just north of Central Park South between Sixth & Seventh Aves., Manhattan

    Known to regulars simply as “The Heck,” this is Central Park's oldest and largest playground, and the default meetup spot for half the families we know. Castle-like stone structures, a stream you can follow up to Umpire Rock, and skyscraper views. Usually packed with tourists and litter, but still very much worth a visit.

    Stump average: 3.8
    Overall Fun: Castles, a stream to follow, and room to run.
    Read the full review »

  8. 8 The Yard, Governors Island

    Governors Island (ferry from the Battery Maritime Building or Brooklyn Bridge Park Pier 6)

    Picture a vacant lot where somebody dumped every old toy, plank, and tire from trash night, then handed the whole thing over to kids. It's muddy, weedy, and gloriously scuzzy — exactly the kind of play most kids never get anymore. Sign the waiver, wave goodbye at the gate, and let them at it.

    Stump average: 3.4
    Overall Fun: A literal junkyard where kids get to play without their parents. Hard to top that.
    Read the full review »

  9. 9 Ancient Playground

    Central Park at Fifth Ave. & E. 85th St., right next to the Met, Manhattan

    Tucked into a traffic triangle next to the Met and built like a brutalist parking garage. Also, the single best place in New York to play tag.

    Stump average: 3.4
    Overall Fun: Unbeatable for tag and climbing, just okay for everything else.
    Read the full review »

  10. 10 Slide Hill, Governors Island

    The Hills, Governors Island (ferry from the Battery Maritime Building or Brooklyn Bridge Park Pier 6)

    Just a bunch of metal slides built into the side of a hill on Governors Island, and it's great because it does that one thing very well. Short fat slides, long thin slides, and the longest slide in all of NYC. Pair it with The Yard for a perfect island day.

    Stump average: 3.2
    Overall Fun: Four slides, three ways up, zero boredom.
    Read the full review »

  11. 11 Tom Otterness Playground

    W. 42nd St. between 11th & 12th Aves. (Silver Towers), Hell's Kitchen, Manhattan

    A giant bronze fellow lounging in a courtyard off 42nd Street, with slides for legs — a supersized cousin of the little bronze guys in the 14th Street subway station. Clean, quiet, and one of a kind. My daughter calls it “The Big Guy and Little Guys Playground,” and I can't improve on that.

    Stump average: 3.2
    Overall Fun: The leg-slides are built for racing.
    Read the full review »

  12. 12 Billy Johnson Playground

    Central Park, near the E. 67th St. entrance off Fifth Ave., Manhattan

    Tucked out of sight from Fifth Avenue and the jogging loop, this rustic little playground is mostly locals, and plenty of parents don't know it exists. A granite slide, a storybook stone bridge with water jets underneath, and a tire swing that turns strangers into friends. A true hidden gem.

    Stump average: 3.0
    Overall Fun: Granite slide, tire swing, and those sneaky bridge jets.
    Read the full review »

  13. 13 Pier 51 Playground

    Hudson River Park at Jane St., West Village, Manhattan

    Like a smaller, newer, more colorful version of River Run, built on a pier. The stream through the middle is perfect for damming with sand and floating things down. Worth a stop if you're near Little Island, but it's small, and I wouldn't go out of my way.

    Stump average: 3.0
    Overall Fun: Damming and boat-floating in the stream is a winner.
    Read the full review »

  14. 14 Adventure Playground

    Central Park, near the W. 67th St. entrance, just up the path from Tarr-Coyne Tots, Manhattan

    A secluded, sixty-year-old classic on a Central Park hill, built from stainless steel, concrete, and sand — without the modern plastic equipment. A long fire pole, hidey castles, a steep little slide, and an epic ash tree. A smaller, quieter Heckscher that locals love.

    Stump average: 3.0
    Overall Fun: A good all-around playground: fire pole, hidey castles, and a little river.
    Read the full review »

  15. 15 Tarr Family Playground

    Central Park at Central Park West & W. 100th St., Manhattan

    Looks pretty ordinary at first glance, but there's a spider web, a little jacks maze, a bridge made for climbing, and a swirly path around the whole thing that kids on bikes treat like a racetrack.

    Stump average: 3.0
    Overall Fun: A solid choice for new cyclists and climbers.
    Read the full review »

  16. 16 Erie Canal Playground

    DeWitt Clinton Park, 11th to 12th Aves. between W. 52nd & 54th Sts., Hell's Kitchen, Manhattan

    A '90s playground in a park with a seedy past. It has a sus rock, sticky mules, slippery floors, and a track ride I haven't seen since I was a kid. Surprisingly nice under the trees, but skip the splash pad and the bathrooms.

    Stump average: 2.8
    Overall Fun: The large park, the rock, and slippery tag make up for a lot.
    Read the full review »

  17. 17 Safari Playground

    Central Park, near W. 91st St. & Central Park West, Manhattan

    Our standard pit stop on a jogging-stroller lap around Central Park. The hippos and play canoes are genuine crowd-pleasers, though a few hippos are held together with duct tape these days. The real star is unofficial: a steep lump at the north end that beats the actual slide.

    Stump average: 2.8
    Overall Fun: The hippos, the canoes, and that steep mystery mound carry the day.
    Read the full review »

  18. 18 Toll Family Playground

    Central Park near the W. 85th St. entrance (Mariners' Gate) off Central Park West, Manhattan

    A clean, fenced-in Central Park playground with one way in, a pile of shiny steel spring toys and spinners, and, for some strange reason, only baby swings. Prime age is 2 to 3. My daughter loved it; I didn't love it.

    Stump average: 2.8
    Overall Fun: My daughter loved it. I didn't love it.
    Read the full review »

  19. 19 Margaret L. Kempner Playground

    Central Park, near the E. 96th St. entrance off Fifth Ave., Manhattan

    Just okay. I'd passed it a hundred times on the Central Park loop and finally went in. The log obstacles and a strange rope contraption make it worth a stop for kids who love to climb, but the entrance is hard to find and there are no bathrooms nearby.

    Stump average: 2.6
    Overall Fun: Worth a stop for kids who love to climb.
    Read the full review »

  20. 20 Pier 97 Playground

    Hudson River Park at W. 57th St., Manhattan

    A brand-new playground right on the Hudson with a big slide you can only reach by conquering a tall ropes course. Fun, and nicely scary. Sadly, a lot of the new equipment is already broken, the bathrooms are always closed, and the staff seem to be on a permanent break. Go for the slide, then move on.

    Stump average: 2.6
    Overall Fun: The ropes-course slide is a thrill. The rest is hit or miss.
    Read the full review »

  21. 21 Tarr-Coyne Wild West Playground

    Central Park near the W. 93rd St. entrance off Central Park West, Manhattan

    A wood-and-sand playground on Central Park's west side that promises the Wild West and delivers a few log stockades. Small-scaled, quiet, and best for ages 2 to 5. The real star is the little river down the middle: great for floating things when it's on, and racing trucks when it's dry.

    Stump average: 2.6
    Overall Fun: The canal is great, but everything else is a little small.
    Read the full review »

  22. 22 Classic Playground

    Riverside Park, on the Hudson River Greenway near W. 74th St., Manhattan

    A playground full of seesaws and a real metal jungle gym — the stuff I grew up on — left to fend for itself. Flaking paint, weeds, missing seesaws, and a dead splash pad. Great concept, sad playground. The city should either give it some love or move the classics somewhere they'll be appreciated.

    Stump average: 2.6
    Overall Fun: The seesaws that remain still work. Bark-stomping is a bonus.
    Read the full review »

  23. 23 Tarr-Coyne Tots Playground

    Central Park, near Central Park West & W. 68th St., next to the Adventure Playground, Manhattan

    A little toddler playground next to the Adventure Playground, with soft rubber lumps that are practically designed for first steps. We came here a lot when my daughter was learning to walk. No rowdy big kids, just calm Upper West Side parents and lots of pacifiers.

    Stump average: 2.6
    Overall Fun: Perfect for brand-new walkers, not much beyond that.
    Read the full review »

  24. 24 Chelsea Park Playground

    Chelsea Park, W. 27th to 28th St. between Ninth and Tenth Avenues, Manhattan

    A fenced-in, mostly plastic playground in Chelsea Park with some crazy ladders, a periscope you can't see through, spitting seals, and a bonus workout area for the grown-ups.

    Stump average: 2.4
    Overall Fun: Decent. Crazy ladders, but only half a lava loop.
    Read the full review »

  25. 25 Imagination Playground

    John St. & South St., near the South Street Seaport, Manhattan

    Deserted on a beautiful Sunday, and the famous big foam building blocks were nowhere to be found. The interesting-looking pipes don't do anything. Maybe that's the joke: the only thing left to play with is your imagination. The boat theme and Seaport setting are nice, but that's about it.

    Stump average: 2.4
    Overall Fun: No blocks, and the pipes don't do anything.
    Read the full review »

  26. 26 Dinosaur Playground

    Riverside Park at W. 97th St., Manhattan

    About half the playground is fenced off because of a sinkhole, and the half that's open is in rough shape. The fiberglass dinosaurs it's named for have holes in them and look like a high school art project. Like Classic Playground, it feels as if the city gave up. The promised renovation can't come soon enough.

    Stump average: 2.0
    Overall Fun: Half the playground is behind a fence.
    Read the full review »

  27. 27 Balsley Park

    9th Ave. between W. 56th & 57th Sts., Manhattan

    The worst playground we've ever reviewed. The fenced-in kids' area holds two lumpy orange things, and it's usually occupied by grown-ups who are not there for the lumpy orange things. I've never seen another kid in there. Lowest ratings across the board.

    Stump average: 1.0
    Overall Fun: Two orange lumps. We counted twice.
    Read the full review »
